## Back in My Day

I remember the time when I just starting out and understood almost nothing on my first job. One thing that I tried was setting a hard deadline for me to try to find answer myself when faced with problem. Usually around 1-3 hours depending on the urgency of the task. If after working on it I didn't find any solution, then I ask my supervisor for pointer or even answer given what I've been trying so far. It worked quite well then, in hindsight I got a lot of privileges
1. Flexible Office (Not really hybrid/ WFH but flexible office time)
2. My supervisor was super open and often entertained my rabbit hole session
3. We had open feedback culture with tight-knit team
4. For technical stuff we had retrospective

All of those contributed to how I built my view about when to start asking question for answer, that is after you've tried your best to solve it first with significant effort or time.

## Am I just Boasting?

Well, partly I am boasting. I treasure my experience in [Labtek Indie](https://playground.knods.app/about-2/) and I must admit it shaped a lot of my work culture of today. But another point is that I want to raise the question of "when should we start asking?". Or in a more nuanced way **"when should we stop struggling and demand to be given the answer?"**. This comes up in my mind a lot after I've been experimenting with using LLM to both help me coding (through [Github Copilot](https://github.com/features/copilot)) and ask a more conceptual question (with a mix of similarly named [Bing Copilot](https://www.bing.com/chat?form=CONVRD) and ChatGPT).

While I felt a surge of productivity increase (measured by features created in a day) and also less distraction (amount of time I "zoned out" thinking for a way out of problem). It seems like all nice and good to the point that it felt almost dystopian, that there's just something that doesn't feel right. That if I continue down the road, there's a huge cliff ahead and I'm being blinded to the point that I couldn't see it.

## What's Right and What's Left

Currently I'm still using these tools to help me daily both personally and professionally. One aspect that I felt the most help is with being able to hash out a quick prototype using some library or technology that I'm not quite good at, and being able to practice "show don't tell" on my idea. 

I occasionally also use LLM to aid me with giving quiz or asking my understanding of something. Yup, I'm not asking the LLM quite the opposite I made it questioned me. While sometimes I got a nonsensical question, but mostly it challenged my understanding of something and prompt me to also search for the answer.

At first I thought that most of my work can be replaced with it and sometimes I asked myself, what's left for me to do?

Actually a lot. I guess I'm a bit biased having come from the time where BERT is the SotA model, but I felt like LLM is good at chaining together words, and semantic. But I still need to guide it to make sure it focus on the right thing, or even ask a specific enough question in order to have a non-generic answer, etc. Well it could also be a skill issue :(

But for now, I myself want to be a craftsman. And honing my skill in software engineering still required me to have a hands on experience in composing code and system. I may ask for help from AI, but not so much that I put them together with my teacher and professor. Maybe a work acquaintance? Someone that I know can do something but I won't take their words and put trust on it without proper verification.
